# The website's design notes

The site at https://mohammedel-sayedahmed.github.io/sedjem/ is one page in two languages: English at the top and
Egyptian Arabic at ar/. build.py makes both from one template, index.html, and the words in copy/en.toml and
copy/ar.toml. These notes are the plan they were built from. The frontmatter above holds the tokens in the form
design tools read: every colour the stylesheet may use, the type ramp, the radii and the spacing scale.
tests/test_site.py checks the stylesheet against it. Change them together with the pages.

## The idea

Sedjem is the ancient Egyptian verb "to hear", written with the sign of an ear; the logo's tile is that ear, and
the wordmark ends in a typing cursor. The page is a quiet, type-led product page whose pictures are the app's own:
the transcript in the hero, written in live HTML, and claim-sized captures of the app further down. The one drawing
is the ear, once, large and quiet beside the headline (smaller, over it, below 1000 px): the brand icon's own paths
as a thin teal line, taken from docs/brand at build time rather than redrawn, and quieter on the dark page so the
headline leads.

Each section makes one claim and proves it beside itself: the claim on five columns, the proof on the seven next to
it, a hairline between sections. The proof is the app (a capture), a measurement (the benchmarks) or a fact (the
list).

## Four principles

1. One grid, one rhythm. Twelve columns; every section one row at 5/7; the caption directly under its proof at one
   offset (12 px); rows further apart than columns. The hero is the one other row: the words on eight columns and
   the ear on the last three, both from the headline's top, the ear's end on the column's end; the transcript runs
   across the column under them, its top inside the first screen.
2. One frame for the app. Everything the app draws sits in the same frame: a tray of translucent ink 4 px around a
   card or a capture, with the card's corners 4 px and the tray's 8 px, and a lit top edge in place of a shadow.
3. Three levels of words. A claim (the h2) of at most two lines, a lede of a sentence or two at 20 px, then the body
   at 16 px and captions at 14 px. Hierarchy comes from size, weight and colour only: there are no eyebrows,
   kickers or uppercase labels, which Arabic couldn't follow anyway. Mono is for data: times, figures, commands.
4. Colour is evidence. Teal in three roles: a fill under white text (the download), an ink for links, and a mark on
   Sedjem's own words and bars. The other models' words are underlined in slate with a wave, as a spellchecker
   marks a word, so the error and the brand never share a colour. The speakers' names are in their colours and
   bold, as the app shows them.

## Grid and rhythm

Twelve columns with 24 px gutters inside a 1200 px column, with page margins of 48, 32 and 16 px. From 1000 px each
section is one row: the claim on five columns, its proof on the seven beside it (from 1280 px for a section with a
picture of the app, which stacks below that so the picture keeps its size). A hairline runs across the column
above each section, 64 px under the section before and 48 px over its heading (48 and 32 on a phone). Below 1000 px
everything is one column, the claim first, then the proof, then its caption.

## Pictures of the app

docs/images/make/screenshots.py's "site" shot runs a throwaway copy of the app on the made-up meeting and takes
each claim's region, light and dark, from a desktop window at twice the pixels and from a phone's at three times,
each window as narrow as the app allows for the region to fit: no wider than 656 px on a desktop, and 280 on a
phone, which is what a 320 px phone's column holds inside the frame. It refuses a region that is wider. shrink.py
then keeps every pixel and colour: each becomes a lossless WebP (method 6) and an optimised PNG, in site/captures/.

The page shows each at its own size, which is the app's: one css pixel of the picture to one of the page, never
smaller, as shrinking is what made the app's 11 to 13 px text hard to read. A picture element offers the WebP and
then the PNG for a browser without WebP; the phone form below 750 px, where the desktop form with its frame no
longer fits the column, a scroll bar included; and the theme tokens show the light or dark pair. From 1000 to
1279 px the seven columns are narrower than the desktop form, so there a section with a picture stacks, its claim
over the picture, as below 1000. Where a region wouldn't fit, the crop is tighter rather than the picture smaller.
The regions:

- the transcript's lines with the Speakers panel beside them (on a phone, the panel, inside its own edge, so the
  frame is the one border);
- Settings, Models: the speech models on the computer, with their sizes and states, and the speaker model in use
  under them (on a phone, the first three models), as the app shows them, Delete links included;
- three lines of the transcript with the line being heard marked (on a phone, without the search above them);
- one edited line in the review before saving, without the dialog around it;
- one line from two models in the compare view, the differing word marked and one line kept.

Each caption says what the picture is; on the Arabic page the first also says the app's interface is in English.

## The facts

The facts under "Runs on an ordinary laptop" are a short spec sheet, after a row of big figures and then a list
with dotted leaders were both turned down: one tonal plate (the tray) with two groups side by side, "On your
computer" (platforms, graphics card, download) and "With your recordings" (languages, what it opens and exports,
the assistants), each term small and muted over its value, and the licence across the foot. One column on a phone.

## The numbers

"Sedjem in numbers" (Sedjem بالأرقام) shows the project's public numbers as a grid of figure cards, the way a
dashboard shows a figure. The owner turned down two earlier forms: the facts' spec sheet again, as plain, and then
a grid of bklit.com's chart cards with axes and dates, as cramped. The claim stands over the grid and a line under it
says where the numbers come from, GitHub's as of their day, with links to the stats branch's charts and to how the
totals are made.

A figure card is its number, large (40 px, semibold, tabular figures), beside its title and one line about it, then
a small picture across the card under them, kept to the card's foot. The card is the card surface with a hairline
edge and, in the dark theme, the lit top edge. Five cards hold GitHub's numbers:

- the downloads in all, with how many came in the last seven days (or since the first day, while the history is
  shorter than a week), over a small line of the running total;
- the latest release's downloads, with the day it came out, over a thin bar of it, the older releases and the
  previews, with a legend of names and shares;
- the larger system's share, with the other's, over a thin bar of the two and its legend;
- the repository's page views of the last 30 days recorded, with their daily average, over a small line of each day;
- the stars, with how many came since the first day recorded, over a small line of them.

The small line has no axes and no dates: a smooth curve (a monotone cubic, which never overshoots a value, so a
running total never seems to dip) over a wash of the same teal that fades downwards, its last stretch dashed, as that
day is still being counted, to a dot, and a ring on a day a release came out. A running total fills the picture from
its lowest value to its highest; the page views start from nothing. The bar of parts is 8 px, its parts 2 px apart.

The sixth card is the anonymous counts' newest month, across a row of its own, so the card before it, the stars,
fills the second row two columns wide: the installs and their countries
large, the transcriptions and hours smaller, the most used model, the five countries with the most installs as ranked
bars and the rest as other, and the website's page views, countries and download clicks. Before the first month is
exported it is one more card in the row, calm and hatched, with one line saying when the first totals come; no number
stands in for one. The grid has three columns from 1000 px, one below; a card whose file can't be read is left out
and the rest keep their order.

The colours are the brand's teal, #14a3a1 (the ear's), for a series and for the part a card is about; a lighter teal,
#6dbeba, for a whole's second part (Linux, the older releases) on the light card, and a darker one, #2f7f7c, on the
dark card; and a grey of the palette for the part that adds up the rest (previews, other countries). The dataviz
validator, validate_palette.js, was run on them against the card in both themes. The teal alone passes every check.
As two categories the pair fails in both themes: the second teal is too close to the first in chroma and, for full
colour vision, in hue. Read as two steps of one hue, which is how the cards use them, both pairs pass every check of
an ordinal ramp; the first light teal tried, #8fd3cf, failed its contrast on white (1.7:1 against a floor of 2:1). So the parts never rely on colour alone: the legend names each with its share, and each card's
table gives every number. Text never wears these colours.

The pictures are SVG drawn by build.py, placed across in per cent and down in pixels, so the words around them stay
at the page's own sizes at any width; the line and its wash stretch across in a nested SVG with strokes that keep
their width, and each wash has a gradient of its own. The Arabic page mirrors the line, so time runs from the right
as its words do. Every number is in the HTML at build time, with thousands separators and in Western digits on both
pages, as the Arabic copy writes every number, each in a data element, each date in a time element, and each picture
has a table of its numbers for screen readers; the downloads' table marks the day each release came out. site.js
adds a tooltip: a pointer or a tap over a line shows the nearest day's value and date, read from that table, with a
hairline at the day, and the arrow keys move it once the line has the focus.

build.py reads the numbers from files (the stats branch's, which pages.yml downloads through the API, and
docs/usage.json) and fetches nothing; Pages rebuilds after each day's stats. A file it can't read leaves its cards
out, and the section is left out when it has nothing to show.

## The benchmarks

"Benchmarks" (نتايج الاختبارات) puts every result the docs publish that a visitor would ask about in one place, after the
facts, which point to it, as do the speaker labels' section: how accurate the transcripts are, how well the English
terms are kept, how fast it is on an ordinary laptop and how well the speakers are told apart, each against what the
project measured it against. It took the place of a table of three models on Perle and of the numbers in the speaker
labels' and the facts' sections, so the page says each number once. The owner asked for it concise, with the repository
for the details.

The claim is the title, a lede with the conditions (public test sets and a private set of recorded meetings, on a
laptop with an Intel Core i5-1245U and no NVIDIA card) and the honest line: a local transcript of a real meeting is a
draft, and a hosted service is still more accurate. Under it, six of the numbers' figure cards, three to a row from
1000 px: on the first row the public test sets (the word error rate on Perle, the English terms kept on Perle, and the
speech TitaNet-small and Nemotron-3-Diarization give to the wrong speaker on ArzEn and AMI), on the second the private
meetings (the words that differ from ElevenLabs' transcript, the minutes for an hour with speaker labels, and the words
TitaNet-small and ElevenLabs' own labels give to the wrong speaker on meetings of two or three people). Under the grid,
one paragraph names each public test set with a link to where it is downloaded and the licence the docs give it (Perle,
MIT; ArzEn, CC BY-SA 4.0; AMI, CC BY 4.0), says the meetings are a private set, and links the results, the speaker
labels and the tables in the repository. The private meetings are only ever that set, with pooled numbers: no meeting,
excerpt or file is named, and the build reads only their pooled rows.

A card's figure is the best of Sedjem's own bars, and the line under its title names that model, and the test set where
the card has two. Under them, bars to compare: one grid for the whole chart, so every bar starts on the same zero line
(on the right on the Arabic page), each row the model's short name, its bar and its value at its end, a heading over a
group (the three models that were also tried, or a test set), and a note under the bars that says which way is better.
Sedjem's own bars are the brand's teal, #14a3a1, and the others, the models also tried and ElevenLabs, the palette's grey,
so colour marks Sedjem's as it does elsewhere; every bar is named, so nothing rests on colour. A share of the words runs
to 100% and the minutes to an hour, drawn as a faint track behind the bar; the speaker labels' shares, all under a
tenth, run to the largest of them, without a track. A bar is drawn to the number at its end, the docs' rounding. No
new colour, so the palette and its validation in the numbers stand. A figure stands beside its words where the card
has 320 px inside it, the card's own width deciding (a container query), so a row is laid out alike, and over them
where it has less. The
line puts the model first; a long name breaks between its words, never at a hyphen, and the line's last words are
kept together, so it never ends on one word alone.

Every number is the docs': build.py reads them with docs/charts.py's own sources, from results/summary.json
(bench/score.py), results/arzen-diarization.json and results/ami-diarization.json (bench/compare_arzen.py and
compare_ami.py) and docs/charts-data.json (the meetings' published aggregates), rounded as the docs round them; the
minutes for an hour are the meetings' mean speed times 60. Nothing is typed into the copy, and pages.yml rebuilds the
site when one of those files changes. tests/test_site.py checks each number against the docs' tables and sentences, the
bars' lengths, the tables, the links and licences, both languages, and that no other section says a number again;
tests/test_ui_site.py checks the layout at 320 to 1440 px in both themes. Each chart is hidden from screen readers and has
a table of its numbers instead, with a caption that says which way is better, in a hidden box, as a table is never
narrower than its words and a long caption would otherwise widen the page.

## Two pages, mirrored

- One template and two sets of words: the sections, their ids, the transcript's lines and times, the registers, the
  benchmarks' figures and the pictures are the same on both pages; only the copy, the captions, the alt text and the
  speakers' names change. The Arabic page names them منى، كريم، عمر، نور. The tests compare the two pages.
- The Arabic page is set for right to left, not flipped: lang="ar" and dir="rtl" on the root; logical properties
  everywhere, so the grid, the bars, the leaders and the menu mirror; the bars start on the right; a model's name is
  one left-to-right run that never breaks at a hyphen, and the preposition before it stays with it; captions under
  their picture.
- Every run of Latin words in the Arabic copy is wrapped in its own left-to-right span, marked as English and not to
  be translated, so names, terms, paths and numbers with their units keep their order inside the right-to-left
  line.
- The language link sits at the end of the header's row on both pages, and in the phone's menu, in its own language
  and script: العربية on the English page and English on the Arabic one. hreflang alternates and a canonical
  address on each page tie the two together.

## Motion

- The page's one orchestrated moment: on load, the hero's transcript lines arrive one after another, as the app
  shows a transcript while it is being written, each fading in and rising 6 px. It runs once, in about two
  seconds, and there is no cursor and no loop.
- The timing is four tokens in the stylesheet, in milliseconds (--motion-start, --motion-step, --motion-row and
  --motion-quick for hovers), each multiplied by --motion, the one switch, which the reader's reduced motion sets to
  0. site.js reads them.
- Every line is in the HTML. Only the script hides them, and only until their turn, so without JavaScript, with
  reduced motion, or for a screen reader, the transcript is simply there.

## Light, dark and the reader's pick

The page follows the system's light or dark setting until the reader switches with the theme button: an icon at the
end of the header's row, or a row with its name in the phone's menu. The icon is the theme a press switches to, the app's
moon on a light page and a sun on a dark one, while the page follows the system too, so it says what the name says;
the stylesheet draws it from
--view-light and --view-dark, so it changes with the system's setting. Each press switches to the other theme, so
every press changes what the page looks like. The name says what a press does, in the page's language: "Switch to
the dark theme" and "Switch to the light theme", حوّل للمظهر الغامق and حوّل للمظهر الفاتح; it is also the button's title
and, in the menu, its name on show.

- A switch to the system's own theme is no pick: the page follows the system again and the kept pick is removed.
  That is how the page goes back to following the system.
- Any other pick is kept in localStorage under sedjem-site-theme, the site's own key, since the pages share their
  address with any other GitHub Pages site of the account; a browser that refuses storage keeps the pick until the
  page is left. site.js puts it on the page in the head, before the page is first drawn, as data-theme with the
  colour scheme to match and the theme-color for the browser's bars, so the other theme never shows first. The 404
  page reads the same key.
- Without scripts the button isn't shown and the page follows the system.
- The dark set is written twice, under the media query for the system's dark (unless the reader picked light) and
  under data-theme="dark", because a media query and an attribute can't share a block; the tests keep the two the
  same. --view-light and --view-dark show the logo and the pictures drawn for the theme on show; the other one is
  never drawn, and the lazy picture never fetched.

## The download

The hero has one download, for the reader's own system: site.js shows Linux's on a Linux computer and Windows'
anywhere else, which is also what the page shows without JavaScript. The link beside it names the other system and
the source, and goes to the downloads section, where both are, with the licence and version line.

Each download is the latest release's file itself, which pages.yml reads through the API when it builds, and the
version line is that release's. Under each button go the file's name and size, as GitHub's release page gives it,
and its SHA256 in mono, which one click selects whole. Without the release's details the links go to the release's
page, with the same words and no file, size or sum.

## The counts

The pages load nothing from other sites, but they count their visits anonymously (docs/privacy.md, "The website").
counts.js, deferred so that it never holds the page up, sends a page_view each time a page is opened and a download
when a download link is clicked, to Aptabase, with fetch and nothing else. Each value is from a fixed list but the
referrer, which is a host name. It sets no cookie and uses no storage, sends nothing when the browser asks for Do Not
Track or Global Privacy Control, and sends only from the published host, which build.py puts on the script's
element with the key, the address and the version, so a local preview sends nothing. The download links say what
they are in data-download and data-place; the hero's is the label on show. Without a key in build.py the script is
left out.

## The share pictures

A link to either page shows its own picture in LinkedIn, WhatsApp, X, Slack and the like: images/og-en.png for the
English page and images/og-ar.png for the Arabic one, 1200 by 630 pixels, the size those apps ask for. Each is the
hero on the light page in a smaller frame: the logo, the headline in two lines, the line of the page's title under it,
the ear beside them as a thicker line, and three lines of the made-up transcript in the same tray and card, right to
left, with the speakers' names in their colours and the English words in English. The Arabic one is laid out right
to left, with the Arabic names. The type is a step up from the page's (64 px headline, 27 px lines, 22 px names) so
that it reads on a thumbnail about 500 px wide, and nothing that matters leaves the middle 1100 by 560, as some apps
crop the edges. docs/images/make/og.py draws both from the copy, content.py, the logo and the fonts, checks that
middle and that no line runs past its end, and saves each as a full colour PNG, packed tighter but losslessly.
og:image:alt and twitter:image:alt describe it in the page's language.

## Accessibility

The same on both pages: a skip link, header, nav, main and footer landmarks, one h1 and no skipped levels; a 2 px
focus ring in the mark teal with a 3 px offset; buttons 36 px tall and 44 px on a coarse pointer; lang="ar" and
dir="rtl" on Arabic lines, dir="auto" on the line that starts in English, and lang on each run of the other language
inside a line; translate="no" on the transcript, the registers, the commands and the product names; a table of
numbers with its caption for every chart; alt text for every picture, and for the Speakers panel the
shares themselves, worked out from content.py at build time; a named, focusable box for the commands that scroll;
and a forced-colours fallback that draws the frames and buttons the theme drew with shadows.

## Review against the critiques and the tells

Round 1 put the transcript under the words, paired sections and cut the speakers' seconds. Round 2 followed a first
outside critique (5/10): one hero column, the transcript right to left, 5/7 rows, an outline ear, CSS crops of the
README screenshots, a four-step ramp, the app's Speakers panel for the bars, and phone and dark fixes; and
Impeccable's rules (registers inset, a still cursor, no eyebrow, a smaller h1, a 404 with its own styles).

Round 3 followed a fresh critique (4/10) and the lessons from uberdesigner, bklit and coss:

- The hero had no centrepiece and an outline ear floating in its empty half. The transcript is now the hero's proof,
  directly under the words with its first lines as SRT beside it; one download for the reader's system and one link
  for the rest; the licence line moved to the downloads. The ear stays, as the user preferred, on the grid: its top
  level with the headline's, its end on the column's end, the drawing unflipped on the Arabic page, quieter in dark.
  The footer's ear is gone.
- Sections shared no grid. Every section is one row at 5/7 with a hairline above it, and every caption sits 12 px
  under its proof; the Perle paragraph is the table's caption.
- Five crops at four widths in five frames. The pictures are new captures of the real app at twice the size, light
  and dark, desktop and phone, cut to each claim and shown at their natural size in one frame; "Read it, fix it,
  compare it" became three claim rows, and its bullets became the facts list.
- Two levels of type and long copy. The ramp has a lede between the claim and the body; claims are two lines at
  most and about thirty words, with the rest linked to the docs; names were ink with the app's colour on a dot; the
  made-up note is under the hero sample and in the footer only.
- A default table, and the brand's teal on the errors. The table was set with care (the benchmarks have since
  taken its place); the other models' words carry a slate wave.
- The Arabic page mirrored by the browser. Its columns read from their start, its model names never wrap inside
  Arabic, its captions sit under their pictures and say the app's interface is in English, and its sample has
  Arabic names.
- The phone's two-row header and shrunk windows. One row with a menu that works without JavaScript; phone captures;
  the commands scroll; the theme icon is a moon or a sun, for the theme a press switches to.
- Dark as an inversion. A surface scale that rises lighter, translucent borders, a lit edge, and the fill teal for
  the download.
- One measure for Impeccable's text rules at 320 to 1440 px on both pages: no line over 80 characters, no one-word
  last line and no unbalanced heading; the ranges (8–25%, 77–90%, 8–9%) never break at their dash.

Round 4 followed the user, who compared the first full version with round 3 section by section, kept round 3's
structure, and asked for these, on the phone too:

- The speakers looked better in the first version. Their names are in their colours and bold again, as the app
  shows them, and the dot is gone; each colour reads on the card at 4.5:1 or more.
- The pictures needed more resolution. They were 256-colour palettes, and a little wider than the column, so the
  browser shrank them; now they keep every colour, are taken at two and three times the size, and are never drawn
  smaller than their own size, at any width from 320 px.
- "Every voice" and "Read, fix and compare" have their crops redone at the app's own size, one border each.
- "Your recording stays on your computer" shows Settings, Models, with the speech models and the speaker model,
  their sizes and states, instead of one row.
- The facts, which the user had turned down in both earlier forms, became the spec sheet above, chosen from two
  new designs.
- And the critic's small fixes: each Latin name one left-to-right run on the Arabic page, "من الكود", the English
  interface said once, the commands' edge fade, and no subtitles on a phone.

After the site went live, the user asked for two more:

- The theme button read backwards: following the system, its half-filled circle looked like the other theme. The
  icon is now the theme on show, a sun or a moon, and each press switches to the other theme, as above.
- The ear was missing on phones. Below 1000 px it is back, smaller, over the headline on the end side.
- The results and the benchmarks in one place, concise, with the repository for the details: the table of three models,
  the speaker labels' sentence and the facts' hour became the benchmarks section above, and their sections point to it.

Checked against the tells: no gradient but the logo tile's own, the commands' edge fade and the empty counts card's
hatch, no glow, glass, blur, grain or orb; no cards in cards, icon tiles or coloured side strips, and a bento grid only
for the benchmarks and the numbers, where each card is a chart; no eyebrow labels, uppercase or monospace labels, or numbering; large
numbers only as a chart card's figure, over its chart; no coloured or italic word in a heading; no device frame or
tilt; one motion; no arrows on links; and no claim or number that isn't in the docs or the data.
